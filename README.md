SOPs to Keep Fire Claims Funded
Build the Five-Evidence Claim Packet
Goal: Create a complete file before documentation gaps slow approval.

Open the claim record with insured, property, loss, policy, and claim identifiers.
Add authorization, assignment, adjuster, and coverage-related documents.
Organize field evidence, including photos, videos, measurements, soot mapping, and safety clearance.
Add contents evidence, including inventory, condition, salvageability, disposal reasoning, and storage status.
Add financial evidence, including estimates, invoices, equipment logs, and supplements.
Mark each evidence group complete, missing, or awaiting review by the project manager.

Control Authorization Before Mobilization
Goal: Reduce disputes over who approved the work.

Confirm the customer’s identity and loss address.
Send the approved work authorization and direction-to-pay forms.
Track delivery, signature, date, and document version.
Escalate unsigned forms to the restoration manager.
Store the signed files beside the claim assignment.
Permit field work according to your company’s approved authorization policy.

Never allow a VA to interpret coverage, promise payment, or approve technical work. Route those decisions to your project manager or designated insurance professional.

Document Smoke and Soot Mitigation
Goal: Connect every mitigation action to visible conditions and job dates.

Collect initial photos from consistent room angles.
Record affected surfaces, residue type, odor observations, and material conditions.
Log air scrubbers, hydroxyl units, cleaning products, locations, and operating periods.
Request daily progress photos and technician notes.
Match cleaning and deodorization actions to the scope narrative.
Flag missing evidence before estimate submission.

Use ANSI/IICRC standards guidance as a reference point. Fire and smoke work now has ANSI/IICRC S700, while S500 applies to water damage from suppression activities. Confirm technical requirements with qualified restoration personnel.

Track Clean, Salvage, or Dispose Decisions
Goal: Defend contents decisions with organized evidence.

Create one inventory record per item or logical group.
Attach pre-cleaning photos and condition notes.
Record the proposed action: clean, restore, store, replace, or dispose.
Add customer or project-manager review where required.
Match disposal records to estimate lines and supporting photos.
Track pack-back or replacement status through completion.

Run the Three-Way Reconciliation
Goal: Prevent mismatches between what happened, what was estimated, and what was billed.

Compare the field record with the Xactimate scope.
Compare the Xactimate scope with invoices and equipment logs.
Compare invoices with authorization and approved change orders.
Highlight unsupported line items or missing documents.
Send technical questions to the estimator or project manager.
Submit a clean packet only after the three records agree.

Maintain the Compliance Desk
Goal: Keep required documents visible during every project phase.

Create a checklist for intake, mitigation, contents, supplements, and completion.
Set due dates for photos, readings, forms, estimates, and customer updates.
Use Google Workspace or Dropbox permissions approved by your company.
Log every carrier, TPA, adjuster, and customer communication.
Escalate safety, coverage, legal, or technical questions immediately.
Archive the final packet according to your retention policy.

FAQ
What is a fire damage claim packet?
A fire damage claim packet is an organized file containing claim identifiers, authorizations, adjuster contacts, photos, scope documentation, contents records, invoices, supplements, and completion evidence. It gives the carrier and adjuster a clear record of the work performed and the costs being requested.

What does a restoration virtual assistant do for fire claims?
A restoration virtual assistant collects and indexes claim documents, tracks signatures, organizes smoke mitigation evidence, updates software records, follows up with adjusters, and prepares packet checklists. The VA supports administration while your estimator, project manager, technician, and insurance professionals retain technical authority.

How should smoke damage restoration be documented?
Document pre-mitigation conditions, room-by-room soot and odor observations, affected materials, cleaning actions, equipment use, progress photos, and final results. Match the evidence to the estimate narrative and preserve communication records. Follow applicable IICRC guidance and your company’s technical procedures.

Can a virtual assistant work in Xactimate and Encircle?
Yes. A trained VA can organize Xactimate estimate support, narratives, supplements, photo references, Encircle records, measurements, contents inventories, and missing-document checklists. Give the VA role-based access and require an estimator or project manager to approve scope, pricing, and technical conclusions.

How does a VA handle contents cleaning versus disposal?
The VA records each item, attaches condition photos, logs the proposed action, tracks customer or manager approval, and matches the decision to the estimate and disposal documentation. The VA does not decide whether an item is technically salvageable or covered.

Who approves fire mitigation work and insurance coverage?
Your designated project manager, estimator, qualified technician, insurance professional, carrier, and policyholder approve decisions within their assigned roles.
